# Docklands Creative – Design Rationale

## 1. Purpose

This document records the design decisions made during the Docklands Creative website redesign.

The redesign was informed by the competitor benchmarking completed during the research stage. The objective was to create a modern digital-agency website that communicates credibility, creative capability and business value while providing a clear user journey from discovery to enquiry.

The design prioritises:

- Clear communication of the agency proposition
- Strong visual hierarchy
- Premium and contemporary positioning
- Easy navigation
- Clear service presentation
- Project and portfolio visibility
- Evidence of capability and credibility
- Strong calls to action
- Responsive desktop and mobile experiences

---

## 2. Design Problem

The website needs to communicate more than the services offered by a creative agency.

It needs to quickly answer four questions for a prospective client:

1. Who is Docklands Creative?
2. What does the agency do?
3. Why should a client choose the agency?
4. How can a prospective client start a project?

The redesign therefore focuses on reducing unnecessary complexity and creating a structured journey through the agency's proposition, services, projects, capabilities, process, insights and contact opportunity.

---

## 3. Design Direction

The overall direction is a modern premium creative-agency aesthetic.

The design combines:

- Dark hero and high-impact sections
- Clean white content sections
- Strong typography
- Controlled accent colour
- High-contrast content
- Card-based information structures
- Generous spacing
- Clear section hierarchy
- Minimal visual clutter

The visual language aims to communicate both creativity and professionalism.

The website should feel creative enough to represent a digital agency while remaining structured enough to communicate trust to business clients.

---

## 4. Visual Identity

### Primary Visual Approach

The redesign uses a dark navy/near-black foundation for important brand moments, combined with white content areas.

The dark sections are primarily used for:

- Hero section
- Portfolio/project section
- Client testimonial
- Final call-to-action
- Footer

White sections are used to provide visual breathing space for:

- Services
- Value propositions
- Process
- Insights

This creates a clear rhythm between high-impact and information-focused sections.

### Accent Colour

A bright pink/magenta accent is used as the main brand interaction colour.

It is used selectively for:

- Buttons
- Links
- Small labels
- Graphic highlights
- Project-card accents
- Navigation emphasis

The accent colour provides a recognisable visual identity without overwhelming the page.

---

## 5. Typography

Typography is treated as a major part of the visual identity.

Large bold headings are used to establish hierarchy and communicate the main message quickly.

The typography system follows a simple hierarchy:

### H1

Used for the main hero proposition.

Example:

> We design. We build.
> We grow your digital presence.

### H2

Used for major section headings.

Examples:

- Digital solutions tailored to your business goals
- Projects that make an impact
- A partner focused on outcomes, not just outputs.
- A proven process that delivers results
- Fresh ideas and insights

### Supporting Text

Short paragraphs are used rather than large blocks of text.

This keeps the website easy to scan and supports users who are evaluating the agency quickly.

---

## 6. Navigation Design

The navigation is intentionally simple.

Primary navigation includes:

- Services
- Work
- About
- Insights
- Contact

A prominent CTA is also included:

> Book a Consultation

The purpose is to make the main conversion action visible without requiring the user to navigate through multiple pages.

The mobile design simplifies the navigation further using a compact menu.

---

## 7. Hero Section

The hero section is designed as the primary communication point.

The main proposition is:

> We design. We build.  
> We grow your digital presence.

Supporting copy explains that Docklands Creative is a digital agency delivering design, branding and digital solutions that help businesses stand out and succeed online.

Two actions are provided:

- Start a Project
- View Our Work

A visual technology/digital graphic is positioned alongside the text to reinforce the agency's digital capability.

### Rationale

The hero combines:

- Clear proposition
- Supporting explanation
- Primary CTA
- Secondary CTA
- Visual evidence of digital capability

This allows the user to understand the agency's offer without scrolling.

---

## 8. Client / Trust Strip

A client or trusted-brand strip follows the hero section.

The purpose is to introduce credibility immediately after the initial proposition.

The section uses a restrained layout so that the client names support credibility without competing with the main message.

---

## 9. Services Section

The services section presents four core service areas:

1. Web Design & Development
2. Branding & Identity
3. E-commerce Solutions
4. Digital Marketing & SEO

Each service is presented as a concise content block.

The design avoids excessive description and instead provides enough information for users to understand the service before choosing to explore further.

### Rationale

The four-column structure allows users to compare the agency's capabilities quickly.

It also creates a clear transition from the broad agency proposition to specific services.

---

## 10. Projects Section

The portfolio section uses a dark background to create a visual break from the preceding white section.

The section heading is:

> Projects that make an impact

Four project cards are presented.

Each card contains:

- Project visual area
- Project/client name
- Short project description
- Service/category information

### Rationale

Portfolio evidence is critical for a creative agency.

The card-based structure allows multiple projects to be presented without creating a visually heavy portfolio wall.

The dark background also gives the portfolio section a stronger visual presence.

---

## 11. Value Proposition Section

The section:

> A partner focused on outcomes, not just outputs.

communicates the agency's broader value proposition.

Four supporting principles are presented:

- Results Driven
- Creative Excellence
- Clear Communication
- Long-Term Partners

### Rationale

This section shifts the communication from "what the agency does" to "how the agency works with clients."

This helps differentiate the agency from competitors that focus primarily on listing services.

---

## 12. Process Section

The redesign includes a five-stage process:

1. Discover
2. Plan
3. Design
4. Develop
5. Launch

Each stage is represented by a compact card.

### Rationale

Showing the process reduces uncertainty for prospective clients.

It communicates that projects follow a structured methodology rather than being delivered through an unclear or informal workflow.

The five-step structure also makes the process easy to understand at a glance.

---

## 13. Testimonial Section

A full-width dark testimonial section is used to introduce social proof.

The testimonial communicates that Docklands Creative understood the client's vision and delivered a website representing the client's brand and values.

### Rationale

The testimonial is positioned after the process section because the user has already seen:

- What the agency offers
- Examples of its work
- Its values
- How it works

The testimonial then reinforces trust before the user reaches the insights and final CTA.

---

## 14. Insights Section

The insights section is titled:

> Fresh ideas and insights

Three article cards are displayed.

Each card contains:

- Image/visual area
- Article category
- Article title
- Short reading/action link

Example topics include:

- How great web design drives business growth
- The ultimate guide to SEO for 2026
- E-commerce trends to watch this year

### Rationale

The insights area positions the agency as knowledgeable rather than purely service-focused.

It also creates an opportunity for future content marketing and SEO development.

---

## 15. Final Call-to-Action

The final CTA uses a dark background and a direct question:

> Ready to grow your business?

Supporting copy reinforces the agency's ability to help businesses achieve their digital goals.

The primary action is:

> Book a Consultation

### Rationale

The CTA appears after the user has encountered:

- Agency proposition
- Services
- Portfolio
- Value proposition
- Process
- Testimonial
- Insights

This creates a logical conversion point at the end of the user journey.

---

## 16. Footer

The footer provides:

- Docklands Creative branding
- Short agency description
- Quick links
- Service links
- Contact information
- Legal links

The footer uses the same dark visual language as the other high-impact sections.

This creates a consistent ending to the page.

---

## 17. Responsive Design

The design was considered for both desktop and mobile.

### Desktop

The desktop design uses:

- Wide content containers
- Multi-column service layouts
- Four project cards
- Horizontal process cards
- Expanded navigation
- Large hero composition

### Mobile

The mobile design adapts the layout by:

- Collapsing navigation
- Stacking content vertically
- Converting multi-column sections into single-column layouts
- Reducing heading sizes
- Maintaining accessible CTA buttons
- Preserving visual hierarchy
- Maintaining consistent spacing

The objective is not simply to shrink the desktop design but to restructure the content for smaller screens.

---

## 18. Design Principles

The redesign follows six core principles:

### 1. Clarity

Users should understand the agency proposition quickly.

### 2. Hierarchy

Important messages and actions should receive the strongest visual emphasis.

### 3. Credibility

Projects, clients, process and testimonials should provide evidence of capability.

### 4. Consistency

Typography, spacing, colours, cards and buttons should follow a consistent visual system.

### 5. Conversion

The website should repeatedly provide clear opportunities to start a conversation.

### 6. Responsiveness

The experience should remain usable and visually coherent across desktop and mobile devices.

---

## 19. Relationship Between Research and Design

The design stage follows the competitor research completed in `01-research`.

The competitor analysis identified common digital-agency patterns including:

- Strong hero propositions
- Portfolio-led credibility
- Service categorisation
- Clear calls to action
- Process communication
- Testimonials/social proof
- Content/insights
- Premium visual presentation

The Docklands Creative redesign adopts these proven patterns while creating a distinct visual identity through its typography, dark/light section rhythm, magenta accent colour and structured content hierarchy.

The purpose is therefore not to copy competitor websites, but to use competitor benchmarking to identify effective industry conventions and develop a differentiated solution.
